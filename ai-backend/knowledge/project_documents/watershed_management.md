# Watershed Management & Hydrology Concepts

## What is a Watershed?
A watershed (also known as a catchment or drainage basin) is a topographically delineated area of land where all surface water from rain, melting snow, or streams converges to a single common exit point, such as a river, lake, reservoir, or ocean.

## Ridge-to-Valley Principle
Sustainable watershed development follows the "Ridge-to-Valley" approach:
1. **Upper Ridges (Recharge & Protection Zone)**: Continuous contour trenches (CCT), staggered contour trenches (SCT), check dams, and intensive afforestation to reduce runoff velocity, prevent gully formation, and promote groundwater infiltration.
2. **Middle Slopes (Transition & Production Zone)**: Farm bunding, vegetative barriers, horticulture, and percolation tanks to retain in-situ soil moisture.
3. **Lower Valleys (Storage & Utilization Zone)**: Masonry check dams, community farm ponds, barrages, and recharge tube wells for irrigation and domestic water supply.

## Common Water Harvesting Structures in JAL IMPACT
- **Check Dam (e.g. WHS-021)**: Small barrier built across a drainage stream to slow velocity, capture silt, and create temporary upstream surface water storage.
- **Farm Pond (e.g. WHS-042)**: Excavated earthen or lined storage basin collecting surface runoff from agricultural fields for supplemental irrigation during dry spells.
- **Percolation Pond / Recharge Shaft (e.g. WHS-087)**: Earthen tank designed specifically to recharge shallow aquifers and raise regional water tables.
- **Contour Bunds**: Earthen ridges constructed along contour lines of equal elevation to prevent soil loss and retain moisture.
